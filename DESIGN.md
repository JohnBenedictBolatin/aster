# Aster visual system

## Direction

Aster is an offline astronaut health console with an immediately accessible browser preview. Its black-and-gray workspace keeps readings, trends, uncertainty, and the next useful action legible. Original gradient icons introduce color and help identify measurements and destinations. This direction supersedes the earlier Tether green palette.

## Adapted rules

- Use a near-black canvas with slightly lifted work surfaces and thin, quiet borders. Separate work through alignment and hairlines rather than heavy shadows.
- Use #080808 canvas, #121212 panels, #1C1C1C raised controls, #303030 borders, and off-white text. White primary actions remain neutral. Amber/red and explicit words identify attention and urgency.
- Use compact, filled gradient SVG icons at 20–24px for destinations and measurements, and translucent layered icons at 40–48px for investigation headings. Utility icons remain monochrome outlines. Do not add colored panels, gradient text, or ambient colored glows.
- Keep unselected category icons in their stable gradients. Use a single white cue for selection: selected icons turn white, cards share a white marker, and tiles in a grid stretch to a common row height. Health status colors remain independent.
- Keep metric colors stable: pulse coral (#ff929f), sleep lavender (#b9a2ff), blood pressure blue (#80bfff), temperature orange (#ffb578), glucose mint (#7ae3bd), mood pink (#f8a0d8). Color means category, never normality or risk. Chart lines match their metric; historical bands remain gray. Legends, line styles, and point shapes supplement color.
- Use the system sans face for reading and controls, Space Mono for compact metadata, and bundled Doto Variable for instrument values and numeric axes.
- Use compact uppercase tracking only for small navigation and metadata labels. Keep instructions, explanations, and health language in sentence case.
- Preserve compact readings, purposeful graphs, the interactive reference anatomy, explicit uncertainty, and the persistent next action. Never imply diagnosis, validated clinical thresholds, or NASA endorsement.
- Keep controls and information dense enough for an operations console, with clear grouping, visible keyboard focus, and responsive layouts.
- Use spacing and occasional separators instead of frames around every section. Keep input boundaries, dialogs, selected states, and meaningful warnings visible.
- Mission log shows one investigation summary and review action, six readings, a trend, a timeline, anatomy, and contextual evidence. It does not repeat indicators or actions already available in the investigation workflow.
- Keep source, method, conditions, mission phase, quality, and rule metadata in expandable record details. Timestamps and verification/stale states remain visible. Source summaries expand in Reference; no scientific limitations or provenance are deleted.

## Source limits

The supplied icon sheets guide material, color, and scale only; the bundled SVG symbols are original. No stock images or watermarks are copied. Existing licensed anatomy assets, scientific caveats, and data provenance remain intact.
